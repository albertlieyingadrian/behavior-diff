title: "Limitations"
description: "What behavior-diff cannot see, and how far to trust what it reports."
---

## Power

By default the tool takes one reply per model per prompt, on 128 prompts. A sign test needs a clear imbalance on
enough prompts. Twenty-five against 10 passes after correction; 5 against 0 does not. A behavior on well under 5% of
prompts will not reach significance. To see rarer changes, add prompts (`--draws 4` doubles them) or items, or write
a targeted eval for the behavior you suspect.

## The judge's reading

Categories, their names and their polarity are a model's reading of the replies. In the demo, a bullet-point LoRA was
named by its consequences ("concise response style", lost detail) rather than its form, because the base already
used markdown lists. The judge checked 29 of 30 planted differences and 10 of 10 identical pairs in the research
note. That is good, not perfect, and a different judge may draw the categories differently. **Read the example pairs
before you act on a category.**

## What it cannot see

- **Multi-turn, tool use, agents.** It compares single replies to single prompts.
- **Inside the model.** It sees outputs, not weights or activations. A trained-in behavior that never fires on your
  prompts is invisible to it.
- **Behaviors outside the suite.** The default suite covers everyday assistant behaviors. Domain-specific changes need
  domain-specific items (`--suite`).

## Statistics

- The sign test treats prompts as independent. The default suite draws each item twice under different conditions,
  so pairs of prompts share an item, which makes q-values a little optimistic.
- Benjamini-Hochberg controls the false discovery rate within one run's categories, not across many runs.
- The same-model control checks the judge and sampling noise for *this* run. It is one sample, not a guarantee.

## Validation so far

The method was tested in [one research note](https://albertlieyingadrian.github.io/steerlab/experiments/behavior-diff/report/):
a password-locked Qwen2.5-1.5B (known answer), its clean twin, an emergent-misalignment LoRA on Qwen2.5-7B, and the
Tinker run. Three fixes were made after reading that data: the within-kind condition test, the two-pass agreed
assignment, and one seed per prompt. They were tuned on the data they were checked on. A second known pair, run once
with the fixed tool, would test them. `bdiff generations` has been tested on synthetic chains only.

## Cost and access

The default judge is a hosted open-weight model, about $0.05 per default run. For an offline judge, serve one
yourself and point `--judge-url` at it. A smaller judge is cheaper and likely less accurate. Rerun the same-model
control to check it.
